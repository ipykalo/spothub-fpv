Getting started

  1. Sign in with Google. Your account is keyed to your Google identity; nothing else is asked for.
  2. Add a build in the Hangar — a name is enough to begin. Add photos from its page.
  3. Add your parts under Parts, with how many you own, then fit them to the build with “Add part” on its page. Record what you paid and the build’s cost adds itself up.
  4. Save the firmware. In Betaflight Configurator’s CLI, run diff all, and paste the output into the build’s Firmware section with “Add capture”.
  5. Import your flights. On the Flights page, drop the radio’s whole LOGS folder — only files not seen before are uploaded. Add blackbox .bbl files and GPX tracks the same way.
  6. Mark your packs. Add batteries as a Battery part with one unit per pack, labelled as written on it, then pick the pack on each flight.
  7. Save your spots. Click the Spots map to add one, or tap “Add location” at the field.

What it never does

  • No tracking. There are no analytics, ad scripts or third-party trackers on any page.
  • Photos lose their location. Every photo is re-encoded on the server and its metadata dropped before it is stored — a photo taken at a home field is a home address.
  • Videos load nothing until played. A spot’s video is a YouTube link, and the player is only fetched when you press play.
  • Maps come from OpenStreetMap, and nothing else is ever sent a spot’s coordinates.
  • Your hangar is yours. Builds, parts, flights and spots are private to their owner unless shared. Only the blog is public.

Who can join

Anyone can read the blog. Signing in is by invitation for now, while the site is young and the groundwork — backups first — is put in place. It is built so that opening it wider changes a setting, not the code.
